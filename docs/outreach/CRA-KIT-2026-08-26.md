## 1. Why it applies to us

A white-labelled / licensed attestation engine sold into the EU is a **"product with digital
elements"** under the CRA. That triggers two obligation families the engine must be ready for:

1. **A software bill of materials (SBOM)** — identify and document components + dependencies.
2. **A vulnerability- and incident-reporting workflow** — report actively-exploited
   vulnerabilities and severe incidents to **ENISA** (and the relevant CSIRT) on tight clocks.

The free / permissionless reference layer is a weaker CRA nexus; the white-label / enterprise path
is the one that clearly makes us a "manufacturer placing a product on the EU market."

## 3. SBOM — CycloneDX

Standardize on **CycloneDX** (widely-accepted machine-readable SBOM format; satisfies "identify and
document components and dependencies"). Generate per shipped artifact, store in the release,
regenerate on every dependency change.

## 4. ENISA vulnerability- and incident-reporting runbook (to be live by 2026-09-11)

CRA reporting clocks (24h / 72h / 14-day) — the runbook is the "who does what, in what order, on
what clock" document.

| Clock | Action |
|---|---|
| **Within 24h** | Report the actively-exploited vulnerability / severe incident to **ENISA** (and the relevant CSIRT) — initial early warning. |
| **Within 72h** | Follow-up: full incident details, the artificial-intelligence/cyber components affected. |
| **Within 14 days** | Final report: root-cause, impact, mitigation, remediation. |

**Runbook owner:** Council of AI (CSOAI). **Vendor to name in the report:** the specific affected
component + dependency.
